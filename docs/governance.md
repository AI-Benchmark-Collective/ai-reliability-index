# Governance and Membership — Draft v0.1

## Mission

AI Benchmark Collective maintains open, reproducible evaluations of AI-system reliability in consequential real-world work. The project exists to produce evidence, not endorsements.

## Roles

- **Participant:** Anyone who uses Discussions, proposes work, or participates in public review.
- **Contributor:** A person with an accepted, attributable contribution.
- **Member:** A contributor trusted with sustained project responsibility and invited to the GitHub organization following review.
- **Fellow or Track Lead:** A member responsible for a defined benchmark track, reproduction effort, or release section for a stated term.
- **Maintainer:** A member with merge or administrative responsibility for one or more repositories.
- **Owner:** A maintainer responsible for organization-wide access, security, legal administration, and continuity.

Roles describe current responsibility, not personal rank.

## Membership review

A contributor is eligible for consideration after two substantive accepted contributions or one major contribution. Eligibility does not guarantee admission.

Reviewers consider methodological and technical quality, reliability in completing agreed work, constructive review, disclosure of limitations and conflicts, adherence to security and conduct expectations, and the organization's need for sustained maintainers.

At least two unconflicted maintainers should support admission when the organization has enough maintainers to do so. During the founding phase, the owner records the decision and rationale.

## Decisions

Routine repository decisions are made by responsible maintainers after public discussion when practical.

Material decisions—including metric changes, official releases, ranking corrections, sponsor conditions, and governance changes—require a written proposal, a reasonable public comment period, recorded votes from unconflicted maintainers, and a documented outcome.

During the founding phase, the owner may make time-sensitive decisions but documents them afterward.

## Conflicts of interest

Contributors disclose employment, funding, investments, advisory roles, privileged access, or close collaborations materially connected to an evaluated system.

A conflicted person may provide factual input but must not be the sole approver of the affected task, score, correction, or ranking.

## Releases

An official release requires versioned tasks and evaluators, a frozen configuration and result manifest, independent scoring or reproduction review, disclosed exclusions and missing runs, a limitations section, conflict disclosures, and approval from the release lead plus one additional unconflicted reviewer.

## Corrections and appeals

Anyone may open a correction request with reproducible evidence. A maintainer not responsible for the disputed decision leads review when possible. The project preserves the original record, publishes the resolution, and versions any changed result.

## Access and security

Repository permissions follow least privilege. Administrative access is limited to people performing active administrative work. Maintainers must not place secrets, private datasets, or unreleased vendor credentials in repositories or issues.

## Inactivity and removal

Elevated permissions may be reduced after prolonged inactivity, security risk, unresolved conflicts, or inability to perform the role. Membership may be suspended or removed for serious or repeated conduct violations using a documented, proportionate process.

## Financial independence

Membership, authorship, task acceptance, and rankings are never sold. Donations and sponsorships must be disclosed. Sponsors cannot veto unfavorable results or purchase preferential placement.

## Amendments

Changes to this document are proposed by pull request and should receive public review. Adopted changes include an effective date and are recorded in release notes or decision records.
